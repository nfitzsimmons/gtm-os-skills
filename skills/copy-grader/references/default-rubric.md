# Default copy-grading rubric

> **Load when:** grading marketing copy in Mode A with no master or domain specified.
> **Do NOT load when:** the copy is a thought or educational social post (use `linkedin-post-rubric.md`), or the user named a specific master or domain (build or load that rubric instead, Mode B).

Twelve weighted direct-response principles distilled from Ogilvy, Halbert, Sugarman, Hopkins, and Caples. Weights sum to 100 and reflect how much each principle actually moves outcomes, so the hook and the offer carry more than mechanics. Score each 0 to 100 with quoted evidence. Weighted total = Σ(score × weight) / 100.

The good and bad examples below are illustrations of the principle, not real data.

| # | Principle | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook earns the read | 15 |
| 2 | One clear idea | 10 |
| 3 | Reader-centered (you, not we) | 10 |
| 4 | Specific over vague | 10 |
| 5 | Believable and proof-backed | 9 |
| 6 | Clear, irresistible offer | 9 |
| 7 | Emotional resonance | 8 |
| 8 | Momentum (every line sells the next) | 7 |
| 9 | Clarity over cleverness | 7 |
| 10 | Single strong CTA | 6 |
| 11 | Rhythm and readability | 5 |
| 12 | Voice fidelity | 4 |

## The principles

**1. Hook earns the read (15).** The first line makes continuing irresistible with a stake, a specific, a tension, or a promise. *Good:* "Your biggest renewal is in 30 days and nobody has opened the account since kickoff." *Bad:* "We're a leading provider of [category] solutions." The opener is the highest-leverage line in the piece, so weight it that way.

**2. One clear idea (10).** The copy makes a single argument. *Good:* a landing page that sells one outcome end to end. *Bad:* a hero that pitches speed, security, and savings in one breath, which is three pages wearing one coat.

**3. Reader-centered (10).** The copy is about the reader's world, not the company's. *Good:* "You'll close the month two days faster." *Bad:* "Our platform leverages AI to optimize workflows." Count the you-to-we ratio. If "we" and "our" dominate the hero, it loses points.

**4. Specific over vague (10).** Numbers, names, and concrete detail beat adjectives. *Good:* "Cut vendor research from 6 hours to 40 minutes." *Bad:* "Dramatically streamline your operations." Vagueness is the most common way competent copy stays unremarkable.

**5. Believable and proof-backed (9).** Claims are credible and evidenced by a real stat, a named customer, or a mechanism. *Good:* "Month-end close went from 6 days to 2," tied to a named customer and a published case study. *Bad:* "Unlock unprecedented growth." An unbacked superlative scores low even when it's grammatically fine. Flag any statistic you can't trace to a source.

**6. Clear, irresistible offer (9).** The reader knows exactly what they get and why now. *Good:* "See your top 3 renewal risks in a 20-minute demo." *Bad:* an offer that is buried, hedged, or absent. No offer caps the score here.

**7. Emotional resonance (8).** The copy touches the real feeling under the transaction, such as relief, fear of missing out, pride, or frustration. *Good:* "Stop being the person who hears about the outage from the customer." *Bad:* a feature list with no stakes.

**8. Momentum (7).** Every line creates a reason to read the next (Sugarman's slippery slide). *Good:* short opening lines, open loops, no dead paragraphs. *Bad:* a wall of text where any line could be the last one read.

**9. Clarity over cleverness (7).** Understood instantly, with no re-reading. *Good:* plain words doing precise work. *Bad:* a pun or wordplay that costs comprehension. If the reader has to decode it, it loses.

**10. Single strong CTA (6).** One unmistakable next action, phrased as value. *Good:* "Get my renewal report." *Bad:* "Submit," or three competing CTAs. Match the CTA to how the product is actually bought. A sales-led product shouldn't promise a free trial it doesn't offer.

**11. Rhythm and readability (5).** Sentence length varies, it scans on a phone, and the whitespace breathes. *Good:* a short line after two long ones. *Bad:* metronome pacing, with every sentence the same length.

**12. Voice fidelity (4).** It sounds like the brand or person it's for, not generic high-conversion copy. *Good:* on voice against the loaded guide or samples. *Bad:* voiceless optimized sludge. The weight is low because voice is the constraint on the rewrite, not the main objective. A rewrite that flattens the voice to chase the other eleven has still failed (see the hard rules in `SKILL.md`).

## Format weighting notes

Shift emphasis by format and keep the weights summing to 100:
- **Cold email or outreach:** hook (1) and offer (6) up, rhythm (11) down. The subject line and the first sentence are the whole game.
- **Pricing page:** clarity (9), believable (5), and offer (6) up, emotional (7) down. Reducing anxiety beats drama.
- **LinkedIn or social thought post:** don't reweight this rubric by hand. A thought post has no hard offer or sales CTA, so use `linkedin-post-rubric.md`. Use this default rubric only for a *promotional* post with a real offer and next action.
- **Homepage hero:** reader-centered (3) and specific (4) up. Whether the hero claims a category the reader recognizes is part of scoring the hook.
